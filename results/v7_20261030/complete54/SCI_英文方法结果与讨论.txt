Government correction strategies and time to belief updating in an LLM agent simulation

Abstract. We examined how response timing, targeting capacity, and perceived government credibility were associated with time to belief updating (TBU), and whether these relationships varied with the degree of group cocooning. A complete 3 × 2 × 3 × 3 factorial simulation comprised 54 scenarios, each involving 300 LLM agents over 10 steps. We distinguished the nine-step restricted mean TBU among agents who believed the claim at step 1 from the three-step restricted mean updating delay after official publication. Scenario-level models used categorical factors, cocooning interactions, HC3 covariance estimates, and Holm-adjusted inference, with wild-bootstrap and matched-permutation sensitivity analyses. Later publication increased restricted mean TBU, whereas higher targeting capacity and government credibility reduced it. Credibility and targeting differences persisted on the post-publication clock; the average timing difference did not. No cocooning interaction on either time outcome survived the planned multiplicity adjustments. Cocooning did, however, amplify credibility and timing differences in cumulative belief burden. These findings distinguish the duration of belief among existing believers from the population-level burden of a rumor. They provide exploratory, model-conditional evidence rather than validation of effects in real populations.



Methods

Simulation design and operational definitions. We analyzed the fully completed V7 batch using a balanced factorial design. Official responses were released at steps 2, 4, or 7. Government credibility was represented by agent-specific prior trust in the government source, with scenario means of 0.25, 0.50, or 0.75. Targeting capacity was operationalized as the within-cocoon share of a fixed audience of 50 recipients: 0.20 corresponded to 10 inside and 40 outside recipients, and 0.80 to 40 inside and 10 outside recipients. Recipients were not screened for current belief. The degree of group cocooning was operationalized as an initial source-trust mean difference of 0, 0.20, or 0.40 between inside and outside agents; network topology was held fixed. Each scenario included the same 300 synthetic personas, comprising 48 inside and 252 outside agents, and used MiniMax-M3 for decisions. A credibility score above 50 indicated belief, and a score of 50 or below indicated rejection.

Time-to-event outcomes. An updating event was the first transition from belief to rejection following the first belief episode. The main duration outcome was the nine-step restricted mean TBU among step-1 believers. All such trajectories were observed until step 10, and agents without an event remained administratively right-censored. The observed restricted duration therefore contributed the full nine-step horizon without being treated as an observed event. A complementary outcome was the three-step restricted mean post-response updating delay among agents who still believed at the preceding step. An update at the publication step had delay zero. Common restriction horizons prevented later-response scenarios from receiving a systematically shorter post-publication observation window. Restricted means were used for their direct time interpretation without a proportional-hazards assumption (Royston & Parmar, 2013).

Statistical analysis. Each outcome was first summarized within scenario, and the 54 scenario summaries were analyzed with equal design weights. The primary categorical model included the four main effects and the three interactions between cocooning and the government-strategy factors, giving 18 parameters and 36 residual degrees of freedom. HC3 covariance estimates were used for approximate Wald F tests and two-sided t contrasts. Eight average main-effect tests across the two outcomes were Holm-adjusted together, as were six interaction tests in a separate family. Twenty pairwise level contrasts formed another family. Pointwise 95% confidence intervals and conservative simultaneous intervals were distinguished. Restricted Rademacher wild bootstrap tests used 9,999 draws; matched-background permutation tests used 99,999 draws. Additional specifications and endpoint subsets were retained as sensitivity analyses rather than selected by significance. Because prior 53-scenario results had been inspected and each factorial cell was run only once, all inference was designated exploratory and conditional on the fitted model, synthetic population, and network.



Results

Data completeness. The batch comprised 54 completed scenarios and 178,200 agent-by-step records, including baseline. There were 911 step-1-believer trajectories, involving 47 distinct agent identifiers, with 712 first updating events and 199 censored trajectories. All step-1 believers were inside the cocoon; thus, the principal TBU estimand pertains to inside initial believers rather than the full population. The pre-response risk sets comprised 948 trajectories. No missing scenario outcomes were interpolated.

Differences across factor levels. Response timing was associated with restricted mean TBU, F(2,36)=139.743, Holm-adjusted p=8.61×10⁻¹⁷. Publication at step 7 rather than step 2 increased TBU by 4.397 simulation steps (95% CI [3.863, 4.930]; pairwise-adjusted p=3.04×10⁻¹⁷). Higher government credibility was associated with shorter TBU, F(2,36)=23.524, adjusted p=1.46×10⁻⁶. The contrast between credibility 0.75 and 0.25 was −2.068 steps (95% CI [−2.712, −1.424]; pairwise-adjusted p=2.02×10⁻⁶). Higher targeting capacity also shortened TBU, F(1,36)=34.197, adjusted p=4.44×10⁻⁶, with a 0.80-versus-0.20 contrast of −1.324 steps (95% CI [−1.783, −0.865]; pairwise-adjusted p=1.33×10⁻⁵). Cocooning did not show a clear average TBU difference, F(2,36)=0.041, adjusted p=1.000.

Post-response updating delay. Credibility and targeting differences remained evident after resetting the time origin to official publication. Their high-versus-low contrasts were −1.290 steps (95% CI [−1.616, −0.963]) and −0.915 steps (95% CI [−1.150, −0.679]), respectively. The average timing difference was not significant on this clock, F(2,36)=1.323, adjusted p=0.837. Earlier publication therefore shortened the overall duration of belief, but these data did not show that it accelerated updating after a response had already been published.

Moderation. Cocooning interactions were not supported on restricted mean TBU: F(4,36)=1.687 for credibility, F(4,36)=0.474 for timing, and F(2,36)=0.895 for targeting; all six-outcome-family-adjusted interaction p values were 1.000. Interactions on post-response delay were likewise not significant. A larger credibility contrast at high cocooning was a directional finding: the extreme-level difference-in-differences was −1.746 steps, with unadjusted p=0.046 but adjusted p=0.274. The corresponding matched exact test gave p=0.03125 before adjustment and p=0.1875 afterwards. This pattern warrants independent replication rather than a claim of established moderation.

Supplementary burden analysis. Cocooning interacted with credibility, F(4,36)=17.526, supplementary-family-adjusted p=2.29×10⁻⁷, and response timing, F(4,36)=26.129, adjusted p=1.95×10⁻⁹, when the outcome was cumulative believer person-rounds. The credibility-associated reduction increased from 28.5 person-rounds at cocooning zero to 123.5 at cocooning 0.40; the burden increase associated with publication at step 7 rather than step 2 rose from 34.5 to 147.0 person-rounds. These results concern the cumulative population burden and must not be relabeled as moderation of TBU.



Discussion

The most consistent findings concern perceived government credibility and within-cocoon allocation: both were associated with shorter updating times across the two time origins and several analytical specifications. Earlier publication also shortened the overall duration of belief, primarily through the waiting period that precedes correction. The lack of clear TBU moderation should not be interpreted as proof that cocooning is irrelevant. Cocooning increased the number of initial believers and amplified policy differences in cumulative burden, indicating that population reach and belief persistence can respond differently.

The findings are constrained by one run per factorial cell, scenario-dependent believer composition, a fixed synthetic network and population, and only 10 observation steps. Credibility weighting was explicitly encoded in the role instructions, so statistical differences partly describe the behavior of the specified simulation mechanism. Targeting represented an audience allocation rule rather than measured detection precision, and cocooning represented a trust gap rather than network segregation. Consequently, the results support conditional comparisons within the model; real-world effectiveness and stable moderation require external calibration and independently replicated scenarios.
